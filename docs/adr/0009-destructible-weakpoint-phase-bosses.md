# Named bosses are multi-part destructible-weak-point fights with telegraphed phases

Each level's final-stage **boss** is a bespoke, named set-piece (BIG MAMA, Double Trouble, …) built as a multi-part structure: the player destroys individual **weak-points** (turrets, engines, gun emplacements — each with its own `_hit`/`_destroyed` art and an **armor** value, so missiles/bombs are the efficient answer), and the boss escalates through 2–3 **telegraphed phases** triggered at HP/weak-point thresholds. **Mini-bosses** (stages 1–2) stay deliberately simpler: a single heavy unit, one weak-point, aimed fire, no phase changes.

We picked this over (a) single-entity pattern bosses (cleaner, but underuses the per-turret `_destroyed` art and adds no target-prioritization depth) and (b) bullet-hell spectacle (off-brand — it fights the noob/mid-tier audience and the forgiving-design stance, and would make flare/bomb mandatory crutches; see [[project-ocelot-target-audience]]). Destructible weak-points are uniquely well-supported by the asset library (modular gun pieces with hit/destroyed states), they make the armor/explosive layer ([[0008-unified-fire-no-air-ground-split]]) and the Spotter/Flare gadgets ([[0007-hangar-four-stat-tracks-plus-gadget-loadout]]) all matter in the climactic fight, and telegraphed phases keep bosses *learnable* rather than reflex-gated — a weaker player beats them by reading patterns and out-investing via the Hangar.

Consequence: bosses need a part/weak-point system (per-part HP, armor, hitboxes, destroyed-art swap) and a phase state-machine driving pattern changes, both reporting to the existing boss-health signals. This is the largest *scripting* commitment per level (parallel to backgrounds being the largest *art* commitment, [[0003-hand-authored-backgrounds]]). Pattern-tier (purple) projectiles are expected here and at elites only. Each named boss should telegraph every attack with enough lead time that the Spotter gadget is a *bonus*, not a *requirement*.

## Refinement — PRD-12 (2026-06-04)

Grilling PRD-12 (#34) resolved the hedges above into firm rules:

- **A boss is a tree of parts, exactly one flagged the `core`.** Destroying the core ends the fight; the non-core parts are the **weak-points**. This unifies the original "weak-points" + "core" language into one reusable `Part` concept.
- **The core is hard-gated: fully invulnerable until *every* weak-point is destroyed.** Chosen over "core always damageable but heavily armored" because the hard gate is the most readable target-prioritization rule for the noob/mid-tier audience ("peel the guns, *then* the core is exposed"). This invulnerability is a **gate mechanic, distinct from armor** — armor (deferred to [[0008-unified-fire-no-air-ground-split]] / PRD-10) only tunes *how efficiently* an exposed part takes gun vs. explosive damage.
- **Phases are driven *purely* by weak-point destruction — there are no HP-percentage flips.** The original "HP/weak-point thresholds" hedge is dropped in favour of the single, always-visible trigger: each phase begins when a named weak-point (or set) falls, and the final phase is *by definition* "all weak-points destroyed, core exposed." So N weak-points yield up to **N+1 phases**.
- **A mini-boss is the degenerate boss: core-only.** Its single target *is* the core (not a weak-point, now that "weak-point" means specifically a non-core part); with no weak-points the gate is vacuously satisfied, so it is immediately damageable — no phases, no peeling. This sharpens the earlier "single heavy unit, one weak-point" wording and means the mini-boss needs **no special-case code**, just a one-part boss.
