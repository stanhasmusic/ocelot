# Unified fire: the player's guns hit air, ground, and sea alike

The player has one offensive attack for targets — the auto-firing forward guns — and it damages everything: enemy aircraft, tanks, trucks, ships, and bunker gun-emplacements. Ground/sea targets differ only by having more HP (and dropping more coins). Bombs remain a separate, limited AoE panic/burst tool that is **never required** to destroy any target.

The decision is governed by one rule discovered while mapping the assets: **anything that can shoot the player must be killable by the player's guns.** Bunkers and ship turrets are active threats (they fire), so a strict "guns-for-air, bombs-for-ground" split (1943/Xevious style) would leave the player unable to answer a turret while out of bombs. We chose unified fire over (a) the dual-attack split (deeper, but fiddly to target on touch and it breaks the shoot-back rule unless guns also hit ground anyway) and (b) treating ground as non-interactive scenery (wastes the large destructible tank/ship/bunker asset set, including all the `_hit`/`_destroyed` art). Unified fire keeps exactly one attack to manage — ideal for the touch + noob/mid-tier audience ([[project-ocelot-target-audience]]) — while making every ground asset a legitimate, optional-but-rewarding target.

**Refinement — armor / damage-type layer.** Unified fire is preserved, but targets carry an armor value: heavy ground targets (bunkers, heavy tanks, capital ships) take *reduced* damage from guns and *bonus* damage from explosives (missiles, bombs). Explosives are therefore the *efficient* answer to armor, never the *required* one — a gun-only player can still grind an armored target down or simply dodge and skip it, so the shoot-back rule holds and no hard wall appears. This gives missiles/bombs a distinct role (they were otherwise redundant) and gives the **Bombs Hangar tier** ([[0007-hangar-four-stat-tracks-plus-gadget-loadout]]) a clear reason to be bought. True gun-*immunity* is permitted only for targets that are always optional **and** avoidable (e.g. an armored coin-vault); it must never apply to a mandatory or undodgeable threat.

Consequence: enemy design is balanced on HP, **armor** (gun-damage reduction + explosive bonus), threat tier, and coin payout — but never on which weapon can *reach* a target. The `_shadow` sprites are treated as visual drop-shadows for depth, NOT an altitude/bombsight mechanic. Ground targets should reward the detour (coins, pickups) without ever being mandatory, so a player who only engages air can still progress. Armor tuning must keep gun-only grind *tedious-but-possible*, not impossible.
