# Progression is a persistent campaign with a between-levels Hangar metagame

The game is an ordered campaign of biome-themed levels. Between levels the player visits the **Hangar** (Airfield) and spends **coins** — a meta-currency earned in missions — on **permanent upgrades** organised into tiered categories (Guns, Armour, Engine, Bombs, Items). Upgrades are kept for the rest of the campaign. Death sends the player back to the current stage's checkpoint, not back to a fresh run. In-level **pickups** remain temporary boosts layered on top and never persist (see [[CONTEXT]] for the upgrade vs pickup split).

We picked this over a **roguelite** (upgrades lost each run) and a pure **arcade score-attack** (no metagame). Two asset facts decided it: the `upgrade_screen` art is built around *selecting and equipping* tiered upgrades on an airfield — framing that only makes sense if purchases stick — and the bosses are hand-drawn, named set-pieces (BIG MAMA, Double Trouble), which implies an authored campaign order rather than procedural runs. It also suits the noob/mid-tier audience (see [[project-ocelot-target-audience]]): permanent power growth lets a weaker player out-invest a hard wall instead of being gated purely on reflexes, and checkpoints keep failure cheap.

Consequence: we need a save system that persists campaign progress + owned upgrades + coin balance, a Hangar screen driven by the `upgrade_screen` assets, and an economy where coin payouts across the campaign are tuned against upgrade prices. Levels must be beatable at the upgrade tier the player can realistically afford by the time they reach them — economy pacing becomes a first-class balancing surface alongside spawn difficulty ([[0002-hybrid-stage-difficulty]]).
