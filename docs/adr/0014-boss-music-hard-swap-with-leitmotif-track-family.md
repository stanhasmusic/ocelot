# Boss music is a hard-swap crossfade with a leitmotif-linked track family; adaptive layering is the deferred upgrade

The named-boss audio moment is a **hard-swap crossfade**: when a Stage-3 named boss spawns, the current level music crossfades out and a dedicated boss track crossfades in via `SoundManager.play_music`; on boss death (or checkpoint-drop that removes the boss) the boss track crossfades back to the level track. This is the only level→boss music transition. Mini-bosses (Stages 1–2) do **not** swap music — the level track keeps playing through them ([[CONTEXT]] Mini-boss). Death and respawn never blink the music either: per the [[CONTEXT]] Life/Checkpoint model, death is punctuation, not interruption, and the music carries through it.

The track-family side: **three boss tracks** instead of one or four — one **shared "encounter" track** for the L1+L2 assembled bosses (Coastal Fortress, the Train) and **bespoke tracks** for the L3 BIG MAMA and L4 Double Trouble hero-sprite bosses. All three are composed around a **shared melodic leitmotif** — common chord progression or theme melody — so the family reads as one *idiom* even when the surface track changes. This mirrors the boss-art split already locked in `docs/design/STATUS.md` (assembled L1/L2, hero L3/L4): the music doubles the visual escalation in the climactic back half.

The surprising-to-future-readers piece is the **rejected default**: modern shmups (and the design instinct here) lean toward **adaptive layering** — a second `AudioStreamPlayer` carrying an intensity stem that beat-syncs with the base level track and crosses in when a heavy enemy or boss appears, so the music *thickens* without ever swapping. We considered it and want it eventually, but it's an asset-class commitment: every level track would have to be authored as a layered stem set (base + intensity + boss) rather than a single mixed file, and the SoundManager would need a stem-mixer fade replacing the current single-channel crossfade. That's a heavier composition load than the first audio pass should absorb. Hard-swap delivers ~80% of the dramatic punch with one stream per slot and zero new SoundManager plumbing beyond what already exists in `scripts/SoundManager.gd`. The deferral path is concrete: once the per-biome music is sitting right, the upgrade is "re-author each level track as base + intensity stems and switch the boss spawn from `play_music(boss_track)` to a stem-mixer fade." The 3-track boss family stays compatible — the leitmotif-linked tracks become the "boss stem" layered over each level's base.

We considered **per-named-boss tracks (4 separate)** — closer to the wrestler-entrance-music ideal — but for a 4-level arcade game the shared L1/L2 track buys real Pavlovian dread on its second appearance (mid-Playthrough) that 4 isolated tracks cannot. We considered **a single shared boss track across all 4** — minimum assets, maximum dread — but it under-celebrates the BIG MAMA and Double Trouble hero moments that the rest of the design (hero sprites, peak-difficulty curve per [[0010-absolute-difficulty-curve-with-hangar-catchup]]) explicitly elevates. The 1-shared + 2-bespoke split is the compromise that matches the design's own escalation pattern. We considered **no music change on boss spawn at all** (stinger only, level track continues) — rejected because the named-boss moment is the level's set-piece climax and deserves a full musical pivot, not a punctuation mark.

Consequences worth stating: (1) `SoundManager.play_music` already implements the crossfade — **no new code** is required for the hard-swap; the deferred adaptive layer is what would require new infrastructure, and is explicitly out of scope until the per-biome tracks ship. (2) The leitmotif commitment is a **composition directive for the user** ([[user-can-author-music]]) — the architecture is correct whether or not the leitmotif lands; if it doesn't, the boss-family still functions as 3 distinct tracks, just without the cohesion bonus. (3) Mini-bosses get **no music event** (no swap, no stinger) — they're mechanically distinct (single heavy, no phases) but musically the same as elite combat, which keeps the named-boss swap as the level's sole musical pivot. (4) On checkpoint-drop during a boss fight, the boss leaves the scene and the boss track crossfades back to the level track via the same hard-swap; the player is never dropped back into mid-boss music with no boss visible.
